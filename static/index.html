    About the model
    • OpenSWMM 6 2D, running on this computer. A 5 or 10 m grid split into triangles; buildings from OpenStreetMap are walls.
    • Terrain: Environment Agency LIDAR Composite DTM 1 m, downloaded for each area.
    • Drawn areas: water leaves wherever the ground falls away outwards across the outline (small dots on the map). Where the ground rises outwards the edge is a wall, and runoff from outside the outline is not included. Mapped rivers and streams entering the outline get an inflow you can set (blue markers); where they leave, the edge is always open (dark markers). The tidal River Tyne is not modelled.
    • Storage tanks, pumps and flood gates are linked 1D elements: a tank's inlet takes surface water at up to its inflow rate into a sealed tank until full; a pump lifts water from a wet well at a low point once it fills to the start depth, onto another spot or out of the area; a flood gate is a wall with one opening per cell along it, open until a control rule closes them all at a set time or when water beside it reaches a set depth.
    • Surface water measures you draw (indicative values for well-maintained features): permeable surfaces soak up to 150 mm/h falling to 40, rain gardens 200 to 50 and pond water, swales 100 to 20 in a lowered channel, new woodland 120 to 25 with rougher ground; all scale with the ground condition and do nothing when the ground is "sealed". Green roofs hold the first 15 mm (adjustable) and release the rest through a 30-minute store.
    • Hazard to people: the Defra/EA method (FD2320), hazard rating = depth × (speed + 0.5) + debris factor (1 where deeper than 0.25 m), worked out at every saved frame and the highest kept. Classes: low under 0.75, moderate to 1.25, significant to 2.0, extreme above.
    • Roads impassable to cars: OpenStreetMap roads (not bridges or tunnels) with water deeper than 0.3 m at some time. Key sites: schools, nurseries, colleges, hospitals, clinics, GP surgeries, care homes, fire and police stations, town halls and railway stations from OpenStreetMap, with water over 0.3 m within 15 m.
    • EA map: the Environment Agency's Risk of Flooding from Surface Water (NaFRA2), England only; it shows the chance of flooding in any year, so one storm will only partly match it.

    • Roofs: rain on buildings drains through a virtual downpipe into the lowest open ground beside each building (not into enclosed courtyards), so the model receives the rain that falls on roofs. Sewers are not modelled, so this is the case with blocked or overwhelmed drains.
    • Flood defences (England): Environment Agency walls, embankments and gates in drawn areas are raised to their recorded crest level (purple lines; gates assumed closed). Natural high ground is already in the terrain.
    • Jesmond Vale preset: gauged river inflow at the top marker and the Ouseburn culvert (9.1 × 6.1 m, inlet control) at the bottom.
    • Roughness from land cover; Horton infiltration on green and garden areas (assumed parameters). Not included: sewers, culverts in drawn areas, channel beds below the surveyed water surface. Results are indicative, not validated.
